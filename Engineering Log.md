Today I started working on **AI Sadhana**.

## My Goal

I want to build a repository that explains **Artificial Intelligence from the ground up**.

The target audience is not limited to computer science students. I want this repository to be useful for:

* School students curious about AI
* College students beginning their AI journey
* People from interdisciplinary backgrounds
* Anyone who is fascinated by AI and wants to understand what is actually happening beyond a collection of algorithms

I don't want this to become just another repository containing implementations and definitions.

I want it to explain the **intuition, foundations, mathematics, code, experiments, and engineering ideas** behind AI in a way that allows a beginner to gradually build a deeper understanding.

## What I Studied

I started by revisiting resources and material I had previously studied.

The purpose was to identify:

* What I already know
* What I have forgotten
* Which concepts are actually necessary
* What order would make sense for someone starting from the beginning

## What I Understood

If I genuinely want to achieve the goal of AI Sadhana, this is going to require a long-term and consistent effort.

It cannot simply be a collection of notes.

I need to continuously ask:

> "What would a person need to understand before they can understand this concept?"

That question will probably determine the structure of the entire repository.

The main questions today were:

* How should I structure the repository?
* What should come first?
* Which topics are actually relevant?
* How much Python should be included?
* Where should mathematics enter?
* How do I balance intuition, theory, implementation, and experiments?
* How do I keep the repository beginner-friendly without making it shallow?

I don't have complete answers yet.

# Engineering Log — 9 October 2026

## What I Worked On

Today I focused on understanding **UAT (Universal Approximation Theorem)** in deep learning.

After studying the concept, I documented it in the Deep Learning Core portion of AI Sadhana.

## What I Studied

- Universal Approximation Theorem
- Its role in understanding the expressive power of neural networks
- The connection between theoretical properties of neural networks and their ability to approximate functions

## What I Understood

The Universal Approximation Theorem is an important theoretical result that helps explain why neural networks can represent a broad range of functions under certain conditions.

However, the ability to approximate a function does not automatically mean that a network will learn it efficiently or generalize well. Approximation ability and successful training are different things.

Understanding this distinction is important when studying the theory behind deep learning.

## What I Built

Added material on the Universal Approximation Theorem to the Deep Learning Core section of AI Sadhana.

## Reflection

This was a useful step toward making the repository more than a collection of introductory programming notes.
