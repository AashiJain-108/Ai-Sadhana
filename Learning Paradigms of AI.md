# Learning Paradigms

**Symbolic AI:** uses explicit human-written rules, logic, and symbols to reason and make decisions.

**Connectionist AI**inspired by the brain’s network of neurons.

**Neuro-Symbolic AI:** Combines connectionist neural networks (for perception and pattern learning) with symbolic AI (for structured reasoning and rule enforcement).

**Statistical / Probabilistic AI:** Uses probability and statistics (such as Bayesian networks, regression, and classical machine learning) to handle uncertainty and make predictions.

**Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Trains an agent through trial, error, rewards, and penalties as it interacts with an environment (popular in game AI and robotics).

**Evolutionary / Genetic AI:** Uses algorithms inspired by natural selection—mutation, crossover, and fitness testing—to evolve solutions or optimize designs.

**Swarm Intelligence:** Models collective behavior of decentralized agents (like ant colonies or bird flocks) to solve routing and optimization problems.

**Generative AI:** Models that generate new content—such as text, images, audio, code, or video—by learning underlying distributions from training data.

**Agentic AI:** AI systems designed to plan, use tools, take actions across steps, and pursue goals autonomously rather than just answering single prompts.

**Fuzzy Logic:** Allows degrees of truth (e.g., "0.7 warm" instead of strictly "hot" or "cold"), historically used in control systems like washing machines, cameras, and transit braking.

**Cybernetics:** The mid-20th-century precursor to modern AI and control theory, studying feedback loops in animals and machines.

**Multi-Agent Systems (MAS):** Environments where multiple independent AI agents interact, negotiate, cooperate, or compete with each other.

**Affective Computing (Emotion AI):** Systems built to recognize, interpret, simulate, or respond to human emotional states via voice tone, facial expressions, or biometrics.
